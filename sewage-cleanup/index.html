Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in Holland, Holland Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Fit a strainer over each sink and shower drain so hair and debris stay out of the pipes
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Toilet water with waste in it soaking into carpet or running across the floor
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
